# Check a patient's vital signs

## Overview

This flow describes how to check a patient's latest vital signs in Care, on the **Vitals** panel of the encounter's **Overview** tab. Each value in the panel is an [observation](../../../concepts/clinical/observation.mdx) recorded in the [encounter](../../../concepts/clinical/encounter.mdx).

## Pre-requisites

- At least one vital sign is recorded in the encounter. If none is recorded, the **Overview** tab does not show the **Vitals** panel. To record vital signs, see [Record observations with a form](./record-observations-with-a-form.mdx).
- You have the permissions listed below.

## Permissions

| Permission | Access |
| --- | --- |
| Can view clinical data about patients | Lets you see the **Vitals** panel and its values. You must hold it through a role on the patient. By default, the Staff, Doctor, Nurse, Admin and Facility Admin roles hold it. |
| Can Read encounter related clinical data | Lets you see the **Vitals** panel of this encounter without a role on the patient. You must hold it in the encounter. By default, the Doctor, Nurse, Admin and Facility Admin roles hold it. |

You need one of the two permissions, not both.

You have a role on the patient when you have a role in one of these places:

- A department of an open encounter of the patient, or a department of that encounter's current location.
- A geographic organization of the patient.
- The patient itself, when someone gave you a role directly on the patient.

A closed encounter gives no role on the patient. An encounter is closed when its status is **Completed**, **Cancelled**, **Discontinued** or **Entered in error**.

You hold a permission in the encounter when you have a role in a department of the encounter. These are the departments the encounter is assigned to, their parent departments, the facility's root department "Administration", and the departments of the encounter's current location.

What this means for the default roles:

- A Doctor, Nurse, Admin or Facility Admin role in a department of the encounter is enough, whatever the status of the encounter.
- The Staff role holds only the permission on the patient. On an open encounter, a Staff role in a department of the encounter is enough.
- On a closed encounter, the Staff role sees the panel only through another role on the patient. Examples are a role in a department of another open encounter of the patient, or a direct role on the patient.

## Steps

### 1. Open the Vitals panel

Open the patient's encounter.

If the encounter shows another tab, select the **Overview** tab.

Find the **Vitals** panel on the **Overview** tab.

A workspace of your deployment can give the panel another title. It can also give the panel its own list of observation codes.

![The Vitals panel on the Overview tab, with a row for each vital sign and a column for each of the four latest times](/media/clinical/observation/check-vital-signs-1.png)

### 2. Read the latest values

Read the values in the table of the **Vitals** panel.

Each row is a vital sign. Each column is a time when vital signs were recorded, to the minute.

The panel shows the four latest times, newest first. The newest column is marked.

The first column of each day shows **Today**, **Yesterday** or the day and month, followed by the time. The other columns of that day show only the time.

A "-" means that the vital sign has no value at that time.

The line above the table names the user who entered the newest value. It reads, for example, "Latest 4 readings · last entered by" and a name.

By default, the panel has a row for each of these vital signs, in this order. On a default installation, the rows carry the names in quotes.

- Systolic blood pressure ("Systolic")
- Diastolic blood pressure ("Diastolic")
- Heart rate ("Pulse")
- Body temperature ("Temperature")
- Respiratory rate ("Respiratory Rate")
- Oxygen saturation ("SpO2")

The row names come from the Primary Parameters chart set of your deployment's chart configuration. If that chart set does not chart a vital sign, the panel leaves out its row.

The panel leaves out observations marked as entered in error.

![The newest column of the Vitals panel, marked with a dot and headed Today and the time; the next column of the same day shows only the time](/media/clinical/observation/check-vital-signs-2.png)

### 3. Check the normal range and the flags

Check the range next to the name of each vital sign.

Next to the name of a vital sign, the panel shows the normal range and unit of its newest value.

The panel flags a value when the interpretation of that value is marked to be highlighted. The flag depends on the interpretation code:

| Flag | When the panel shows it |
| --- | --- |
| **Above normal range** | The interpretation code starts with H (high). |
| **Below normal range** | The interpretation code starts with L (low). |
| **Abnormal** | The interpretation is highlighted and its code starts with another letter or is empty. |

Only a test result entered on a diagnostic report can have a normal range and an interpretation. Care computes them from the test's [observation definition](../../../concepts/definitions/observation-definition.mdx) when the result is saved.

A vital sign recorded with a questionnaire has no normal range and no interpretation. Its row shows no range, and its values are never flagged.

![The Temperature row of the Vitals panel, with its unit next to the name and no normal range](/media/clinical/observation/check-vital-signs-3.png)

### 4. Show only the flagged vital signs

If at least one value in the panel is flagged, the panel shows the **Abnormal only** switch.

Turn on **Abnormal only** to show only the rows with a flagged value.

### 5. Open the history of a vital sign

To see the history of all the vital signs in the panel, select **All readings**.

To see the history of one vital sign, select its name.

Care opens the history panel. To read it, see [Chart observations over time](./chart-observations-over-time.mdx).

![The Vitals history panel opened with All readings, showing All Encounters, 48 recordings, the Plots and Table tabs, and a chart for each vital sign](/media/clinical/observation/check-vital-signs-4.png)

![The Temperature history panel opened from the name of the vital sign, showing All Encounters, 8 recordings and a chart of Body temperature](/media/clinical/observation/check-vital-signs-5.png)

## Expected Outcome

- You see the vital signs of the encounter at up to four times, newest first.
- Where the newest value of a vital sign has a normal range, the row shows it with the unit.
- Flagged values show **Above normal range**, **Below normal range** or **Abnormal**.
- You can open the history panel of one vital sign or of all of them.

If you see one of these situations, use this table.

| If you see | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The **Overview** tab has no **Vitals** panel | No vital sign is recorded in this encounter yet. | Record the vital signs. See [Record observations with a form](./record-observations-with-a-form.mdx). |
| "You do not have permission to view clinical data for this encounter" on the **Overview** tab | You hold neither permission listed above in the right place. | Ask the person who manages user roles in your facility for one of the permissions listed above. |
| "You do not have permission to view this encounter", and Care goes back | You can neither read the clinical data of this encounter nor read the encounter. | Ask the person who manages user roles in your facility for one of the permissions listed above. |
| A vital sign has no normal range and no flag | The values were recorded with a questionnaire, or the test's definition gave them no range. | Compare the value with the normal range yourself. A missing flag does not mean that the value is normal. |
